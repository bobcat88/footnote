What a result contains

  • Where. File path, heading, page and line.
  • Which bytes. A SHA-256 of the exact text the result came from, and a revision number.
  • Still true? current, stale (the file changed since it was indexed) or missing_or_unreadable.
  • Read-only. An agent can recall and fetch. It cannot write to your documents. Every call goes into a tamper-evident audit log.

The documentation of your stack

footnote docs ingest --project . finds the libraries your project imports and indexes their docs, the most used first. Search works as soon as the keyword index exists; meaning-based search switches on by itself when the background embedding finishes.

  • Time to first answer. 8 libraries, 507 pages, one run on CPU, 20 Nov 2026: the first search worked after 5.9 s and all eight were searchable by keyword after 44.1 s. Embedding finished at 567.5 s. The total did not get shorter than before (548 s), so we claim the early answer, not a faster ingest.
  • Against Grounded Docs 3.4.1. Crawl and index: 10.1 s against 160.6 s on the Scrapy docs, 7.6 s against 121.8 s on the pytest docs. Median query 1.15 s against 1.83 s. Two sites, 40 questions, a protocol sealed before the run. Footnote stored 200 pytest pages against 95.
  • Quality against Grounded Docs: inconclusive. On those 40 questions nDCG@5 was 0.768 against 0.721, inside the noise (95% interval -0.054 to +0.155). On an earlier set of 59 questions we scored lower, 0.564 against 0.635.
  • Prebuilt docs bundles. The command exists. None are shipped or hosted yet.

  • Published reference. BM25 from the BEIR paper scores 0.665 on SciFact and 0.325 on NFCorpus.
  • Caveats. We ran every tool ourselves. mem0 ran without LLM extraction, Cognee on its chunk index, ICM keyword-only. Cognee failed on the mixed set. Public test sets may overlap with data the models were trained on. Competitor versions are snapshots.
  • Where we lose. Building the index takes Footnote 280 s against 162 s for mem0 on SciFact, and 712 s against 336 s on the mixed set. ICM builds 6 to 7 times faster.

Speed

  • Search. Median 20 ms at 5,183 chunks, 45 ms at 40,018 and 52 ms at 100,044 (95th percentile 79 ms), end to end over MCP, 300 real queries. The two larger rows use random vectors: they show speed, not precision.
  • Keyword index alone. 3.0 ms against 423 ms for SQLite FTS5 at 1,000,000 synthetic documents cut from real text. Speed only; the two stores are not the same size or content.

Limits

  • The English stemmer is also used on French text. It beat the alternatives we tried on a mixed index.
  • We have not measured precision at 100,000 chunks.
  • PDF text and OCR need the Python helpers.
  • Meaning-based search needs the two models (about 200 MB). Without them you still get keyword search.
